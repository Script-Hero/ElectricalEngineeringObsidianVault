## Chapter 5 -- Applications of Gauss' Law 
- $\oint_{S}\vec{E}\cdot d\vec{S} = \frac{Q}{\epsilon_{0}}$
- Most important part is choosing correct surface $S$
- We usually choose $S$ based off some fact about $\vec{E}$ the [[Electric Field]]
	- We like Gaussian Spheres because most E-Fields point radially outwards

**Insulators** do not allow charges to move, and we can ignore the charges of particles inside insulators

**[[Conductors]]** allow charges to move, and we have to consider them in problems.

## Chapter 6 -- [[Capacitors]] 
- A battery creates a constant [[Physics E&M/EXAM-1/Electric Potential|Electric Potential]] difference  $\Delta V$ between its 2 terminals

On a *parallel plate capacitor* capacitance $C = \frac{Q}{\Delta V}=\frac{A\epsilon_{0}}{L}$
or $Q = CV$
- A larger $C$ means a larger capacity to hold charges
- Unit is *farad* = $\frac{Coulomb}{Volts}=f$
	- $\mu f=10^{-6}f$
	- $pf=10^{-12}f$
- Definitionally positive, $C=|C|$
- We add [[Capacitors]] *in series* with reciprocals:
	- $\frac{1}{C}=\frac{1}{C_{1}}+\frac{1}{C_{2}}+\frac{1}{C_{3}}+...$
- *In parallel*
	- $C=C_{1}+C_{2}+C_{3}+...$
- Energy stored in a capacitor:
	  $W=\frac{1}{2C}Q^{2}=\frac{1}{2}CV_{tot}^2$
	
## Chapter  7 -- [[Current]], Ohm's Law, Etc. 
- $\frac{V}{i}=R$
	- $V =$ [[Voltage]]
	- $i =$ [[Current]]
	- $R =$ resistance ***which is a constant***
Resistance of a wire:
$R = \rho\frac{l}{A}$
- $\rho$ is constant of proportionality (called *resistivity*)
- $l$ is wire length
- $A$ is area of wire

*Conductivity*, $\sigma$, is $\frac{1}{\rho}$

- [[Current]] of wire connected to battery $i=\frac{V}{R}=\frac{V}{\frac{\rho l}{A}}=\frac{VA}{l}\frac{1}{\rho}=\frac{VA}{l}\sigma$
