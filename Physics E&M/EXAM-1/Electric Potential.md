Any electric force produced by stationary charges is conservative.

* $-\int^{r_2}_{r_1}\vec{F}=\vec{U}$

Sometimes it's easier to use the radial coordinate system, parameterized by $r$ and $\theta$, because it can eliminate a component of the force and simplify the integral.

*Coulomb Potential:* $\vec{U}(r)=\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{qq_0}{r}$
- This is the electric potential due to a Coulomb Force

We can calculate the *electric potential* $\vec{V}(x,y)$ by performing the same limit with the test charge as we do to find the force at a point in an [[Electric Field]].

We can also find the force that 2 electrons have by integrating, finding the potential, adding the potentials, and then deriving them back into forces.
