You can measure the electric field created by multiple charges by placing a "test charge" that is infinitesimally small (as to not have an effect on other charges in the system) and then dividing out that infinitesimally small test charge at the end to just get the magnitude of the force at that point.

- In practice this usually means that the equation $\vec{F}=\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{qq_0}{r^2}$ where $q_0$ is an infinitesimally small test charge is converted into the electric field equation as $\vec{E}=\frac{\vec{F}}{q_0}=\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{q}{r^2}$ 

In problems where a charge is moving through a field created by *fixed* charges, we can find the force acting upon the charge as $F=q\vec{E}$
