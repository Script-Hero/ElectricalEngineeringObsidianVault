## Legislative Committees: Structure and Dynamics

---

### Functions
Most legislative chambers (like state legislatures or the U.S. Congress) maintain **20 to 30 standing committees**. These committees handle **specific areas of policy or governance**—such as education, health, transportation, or finance—and are essential for reviewing, amending, and filtering proposed legislation before it reaches the full chamber.

---

### Personnel
**Committee assignments** are typically made by the **chamber’s leadership** (e.g., Speaker of the House or Senate President). While political considerations play a major role, **occupational background** or subject-matter expertise of members may influence where they are placed (e.g., a former teacher on the Education Committee).

---

### Committee Preferences
Committee assignments usually **reflect the policy interests and preferences of the chamber’s members**. Lawmakers often request committees that align with their constituency’s needs, personal expertise, or political goals. This alignment helps ensure that members are actively engaged and effective within their roles.
