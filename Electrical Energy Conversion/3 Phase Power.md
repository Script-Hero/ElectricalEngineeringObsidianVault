A **balanced** three-phase ($\phi$) system:
- Three [[Voltage]] sources
	- Angle shift of $120\degree$
- Equal:
	- Loads on each phase Magnitude
	- [[Current]]
	- Impedance on lines connecting the generators to the loads

# Assumptions when Balanced
- No neutral [[Current]]
	- $\bar{I}_n=\bar{I}_a+\bar{I}_b+\bar{I}_c$
		- $=\frac V Z(1\angle0\degree+1\angle-120\degree+1\angle120\degree)=0A$

- No line impedance 
- [[Power]] (instantaneous, average, and complex) is the sum of the powers of the three phases
	- $p(t)=p_a(t)+p_b(t)+p_c(t)$
	- $P=P_a+P_b+P_c$
	- $Q=Q_a+Q_b+Q_c$
	- $S=(P_a+jQ_a)+(P_b+jQ_b)+(P_c+jQ_c)$

# Advantages
- Can transmit more [[Power]] for the same amount of wire
	- Twice as much as single phase
- Torque produced by 3$\phi$ machines is constant
- 3$\phi$ use less material for same [[Power]] rating
- 3$\phi$ machines start more easily than single-phase machines
