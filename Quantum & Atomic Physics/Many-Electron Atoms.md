### Hund's Rules
1. The *total spin angular momentum* $S$ (see [[Magnetic Spin Quantum Number]]) should be maximized to the extent possible without violating the [[Pauli Exclusion Principle]].
	- Called the "bus seat rule", analogous to bus passengers occupying all double seats singly before double occupation occurs.
2. As long as Rule 1 is not violated, *total orbital angular momentum* $L$ (see [[Orbital Angular Momentum Quantum Number]]) should also be maximized
	- Deals with repulsion between electrons. Picturing all the electrons orbiting in the same direction, they'll boink into each other less than if some were going in the opposite direction of others. In the boinking situation, they'll be pushed in direction causing them to seperate, adding potential energy, and thereby raising energy levels
1. For atoms having subshells less than half full, $J$, the [[Total Angular Momentum]], should be *minimized*.
		- Considers the energy shift between [[Spin-Orbit Coupling]]. If [[Spin-Orbit Coupling]] is weak compared to the residual electrostatic interaction, $L$ and $S$ are still good [[Quantum Number]]s and the splitting is given by minimizing $J$ .  

#### For labeled two-electron atom
$\vec J = \vec L_1 + \vec L_2 + \vec S_1 + \vec S_2$
- There are [[LS Coupling]] and [[jj Coupling]] coupling to combine four angular momenta $J$
- in $jj$ coupling, $J=L+S$ for each electron
