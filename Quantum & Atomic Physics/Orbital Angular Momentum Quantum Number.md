[[Quantum Number]] $l$ -- can be any integer *greater than or equal to* 0. -- **primarily describes shape of electron's orbit**

- Associated with $R(r)$ and $f(\theta)$ parts of the wave function
- The orbital angular moment $\vec{L}=mv_{\mbox{orbital}}r$ can also be described **in terms of $l$**:
	- $L=\sqrt{l(l+1)}\hbar$
	- this result disagree with Bohr's semi-classical "planetary" model of electrons orbiting a nucleus using $L=n\hbar$

An energy level is considered **degenerate** with respect to $l$ when the **energy level** is independent of $l$
- *degeneracy* in context of [[Hydrogen Atom]] is the $2l+1$ states (from $m_l=-l$ to $m_l=+l$) that have the same energy level
	- this means that the energy level *depends only on* $n$, (it is *independent of $l$*).
- this is due to the [[Hydrogen Atom]] being roughly "spherically symmetric" 
	- Some things can impact this symmetry and cause ***non****degeneracy*
		1. Magnetic Field -- [[Normal Zeeman Effect]]
		2. [[Electric Field]] -- 

Using letter names for the various $l$ values:

| $l$ | Letter |
| :-: | :----: |
|  0  |  $s$   |
|  1  |  $p$   |
|  2  |  $d$   |
|  3  |  $f$   |
|  4  |  $g$   |
|  5  |  $h$   |

**Atomic States are referred to by their $n$ and $l$.**
- A state with $n=2$ and $l=1$ is called $2p$ state
- The [[Wave Function Boundary Conditions]] requires $n>l$
