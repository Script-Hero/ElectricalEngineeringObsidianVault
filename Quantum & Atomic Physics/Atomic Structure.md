### Atomic Structure
- **[[Hydrogen Atom]]**: ($n$, $l$, $m_l$, $m_s$) = $(1,0,0,\pm\frac{1}{2})$ in ground state
	- In the absence of a magnetic field, the state $m_s=\frac{1}{2}$ is *degenerate* with the $m_s=-\frac{1}{2}$
	- Electrons are in the K Shell
		- $1s^1$ or just $1s$
- **Helium Atom**: 
	- $(1,0,0,\frac{1}{2})$ for the first electron
	- $(1,0,0,-\frac{1}{2})$ for the second electron
	- Electrons have anti-aligned ($m_s=+\frac{1}{2}$ and $m_s=-\frac{1}{2}$) spins as being *paired* --> supports the [[Pauli Exclusion Principle]]
	- Electrons are in the K Shell
		- $1s^2$

### Shells
- [[Principal Quantum Number]] $n$ has letter codes, representing the *shells* 
	- "K Shell"
	- "L Shell"
- [[Principal Quantum Number]] and [[Orbital Angular Momentum Quantum Number]] $nl$ letter codes represent *subshells*
	- $1s$
	- $2p$ (representing $n=2$ and $l=1$)
	- $3d$

### How many electrons in each subshell?
- For each $m_l$ [[Magnetic Quantum Number]]: two values of $m_s$ [[Magnetic Spin Quantum Number]] **$\rightarrow +2$ electrons.**
- For each [[Orbital Angular Momentum Quantum Number]] $l$: $(2l + 1)$ values of $m_l$ **$\rightarrow +2(2l+1)$ electrons.**
##### Formula for electrons per subshell $2m_l + 2(2l+1)$
- So:
	- $l=0$ (s state) can have **2 electrons**
	- $l=1$ (p state) can have **6 electrons** and so on
### Order of Electrons Filling Atomic Subshells
- The lower $l$ values have more elliptical orbits than the higher $l$ values
	- Electrons with higher $l$ values are more shielded from the nuclear charge
		- **This shielding is so pronounced that the $4s$ fills before $3d$ even though it has a larger $n$.**
	- Electrons with higher $l$ values have more energy than those with lower $l$ values
