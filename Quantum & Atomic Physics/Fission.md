- Nucleus separates into 2 fragments, one usually bigger
- Occurs more for heavy nuclei w greater Coulomb force (more electronegative)
	- As a molecule becomes less like the spherical [[Hydrogen Atom]] short range interactions are reduced, when $A\sim240$
	- Can be understood using the [[Liquid Drop Model]]
- Nucleons on the surface of the atom are not protected by other nucleons, and this empty space acts as a barrier, but at a certain level this "barrier" is overcome
	- Spontaneous fission occurs when [[Atomic Number]]$^2 / A \geq 49$


#### Induced Fission
- A neutron absorbed by a radioactive particle forms a very excited compound which then does fission
- Ratio of neutrons / [[Atomic Number]] too high to be stable
- Many possible outcomes
	- Likely one mass larger than the other when they fission split

#### Thermal Fission
- Fission fragments are neutron rich = unstable
- Even after prompt neutrons (released simultaneously as fission goes) are released, fission fragment beta decay release more energy
	- Because neutrons are released during fission, they could also be absorbed by the fission components, causing another fission all the way down to a chain react
- If, on average, one neutron per fission causes another fission --> reaction is *critical*
	- > 1 --> supercritical
	- < 1 --> subcritical
- suffecient mass of fission particle to even absorb a neutron is critical mass

#### Fission Reactors
- Not all neutrons are *prompt*, some are *delayed* meaning they come from the daughter nuclei
	- Controls reaction (makes it sustainable) in a reactor
- *[[Control]] Rod* in reactor regulate this stuff
