2. A particular accelerator can accelerate electrons to an energy of 34.0 GeV. What is the de Broglie wavelength (in m) of these electrons? What fraction of a proton's diameter can such a particle probe?
	1. $\lambda = \frac{\hbar}{p}$ (de broglie wavelength)
	2. $p=\frac{E}{c}$ [[Relativistic Momentum]]
	3. probe fraction = $\frac{\lambda}{d}$
3. Find the kinetic energy (in eV) of the following particles that each have a de Broglie wavelength of 0.360 nm.

	2. $K=\frac{p^{2}}{2m}$ kinetic energy from [[Relativistic Momentum]] $\rightarrow K=\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m_{n}\lambda ^ 2}$
	3. for non-relativistic particles, $p=\sqrt{2mK}$

6. An atom in an excited state of 4.20 eV emits a photon and ends up in the ground state. The lifetime of the excited state is 2.30 ✕ 10−13 s. What is the energy uncertainty of the emitted photon and what is the spectral line width of the photon?

	2. Solve for the spectral wavelength with $E=\frac{\hbar c}{\lambda}\rightarrow \Delta \lambda \approx \frac{\lambda^{2}\cdot\Delta E}{\hbar c}$

8. Calculate the de Broglie wavelength (in fm) of a 5.60 MeV 𝛼 particle emitted from an atomic nucleus whose diameter is approximately 1.90 ✕ 10−14 m.
