#Chapter8 

### Lore
- What would happen if there are *more than one electron?*
	- A nucleus with charge $+2e$ attracting two electrons?
	- The two electrons repelling one another?
- Could not solve these problems exactly using the [[Shrodinger Equation]] because of the complex potential interactions
- You can also understand experimental results without computing the wave function of many-electron atoms by applying the [[Wave Function Boundary Conditions]] and [[Energy Transitions]] selection rules.
- To understand atomic spectroscopic data for optical frequencies, Wolfgang Pauli proposed an *exclusion principle*:

### **Definition:** No two electrons in an atom may have the same set of [[Quantum Number]] ($n$, $l$, $m_l$, $m_s$).
- Applies to all particles with half-integer [[Magnetic Spin Quantum Number]]
	- called *fermions*
	- all particles in the nucleus are fermions

### Application to the [[Periodic Table]]
Only one electron can be in in a state with a given (complete) set of quantum numbers.
