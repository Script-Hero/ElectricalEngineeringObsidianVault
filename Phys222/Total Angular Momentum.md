Total Angular Momentum $\vec J$ is equal to the sum of Orbital Angular Momentum (see [[Orbital Angular Momentum Quantum Number]]) and Spin Angular Momentum (see [[Magnetic Spin Quantum Number]]):
- $\vec J = \vec L + \vec S$
	- $L, L_z, S, S_z, J, J_z$ are all quantized

### For Single Electron Atoms Outside an Inert Core (like the [[Hydrogen Atom]])
- If $j$ and $m_j$ are [[Quantum Number]]s for the single electron ([[Hydrogen Atom]]):
	- $J = \sqrt{j(j+1)\hbar}$
	- $J_Z=m_j\hbar$
- Quantization of the magnitudes:
	- $L=\sqrt{l(l+1)}\hbar$
	- $S=\sqrt{s(s_1)}\hbar$
	- $J=\sqrt{j(j+1)}\hbar$
- The  [[Total Angular Momentum Quantum Number]] $j$ for the single electron can only have the values $j=l\pm s$

*When forming the total angular momentum from the orbital and spin angular momenta, the addition must be done vectorally, $j=l+s$*
