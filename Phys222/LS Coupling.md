- Interaction between the magnetic spin momentum $S$ and orbital angular momentum $L$ between electrons in [[Many-Electron Atoms]]
- Used for most atoms when the magnetic field is weak

![[ls_coupling.png]]

- If 2 electrons are in a single subshell, $S=0$ or $1$ depending on whether the [[Magnetic Spin Quantum Number]] are parallel or anti-parallel.

#### Multiplicity
- For given orbital angular momentum $L$ there are $2S+1$ values of $J$
- For $L>S$, possible values of $J$ go from $L-S$ to $L+S$
- For $L<S$, there are fewer than $2S+1$ possible values of $J$
- The value of $2S + 1$ is the **multiplicity** of the state

### Notation
- The notation for a single-electron atom becomes
	- $n^{2S+1}L_J$
- The letters and numbers are called *spectroscopic symbols*
- There are *singlet* states ($S=0$) and *triplet* states ($S=1$) for two electrons

### LS Coupling for the *magnesium atom*
- There are separated energy levels according to whether they are $S=0$ or $1$
- Allowed transitions must have:
	- $\Delta S = 0$
	- $\Delta L = \pm 1$
	- $\Delta J=0,\pm1$ (*but* $J=0\rightarrow J=0$ *is forbidden* )
- No allowed transitions are possible between *singlet* and *triplet* states
- A magnesium atom excited to the $3s3p$ *triplet* state has no lower *triplet* state to which it can decay
- $3p$ state is "metastable" because it lives for such a long time on the atomic scale
