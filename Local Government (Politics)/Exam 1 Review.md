**1) Know which level of government is responsible for which different policies**  
Typically, the federal government handles matters of national defense, foreign policy, interstate commerce, and overarching civil rights protections. State governments oversee public education, health, safety, and intrastate commerce, while local governments are responsible for services like policing, fire departments, zoning, and local road maintenance.

---

**2) What is policy conservatism?**  
Policy conservatism generally favors limited government intervention in economic affairs and places emphasis on individual responsibility. It also tends to support lower taxes, reduced spending on social programs, and a focus on traditional social values.

---

**3) What is policy liberalism?**  
Policy liberalism typically advocates for an active role of government in addressing social and economic issues. It promotes expanded social welfare programs, progressive taxation, and policies aimed at reducing social inequalities.

---

**4) From where do state and local governments derive their revenue?**  
State and local governments receive revenue primarily through taxation (property, sales, and income taxes, depending on the state), fees (such as vehicle registration or licenses), and intergovernmental transfers such as federal grants. They may also rely on bonds, lotteries, and other specialized funding mechanisms.

---

**5) Know the different types of government systems (federalism, unitary, confederation)**  
- **Federalism:** Power is shared between a central (national) government and regional (state) governments.  
- **Unitary:** All major powers and policy decisions are concentrated in the central government, with local entities existing primarily to implement national policies.  
- **Confederation:** A loose alliance of independent states in which the central government’s authority is limited, and the member states retain most powers.

---

**6) Why are states called “laboratories of democracy?”**  
States are often referred to as “laboratories of democracy” because they can experiment with innovative policies on a smaller scale. Successful programs can then be replicated at the national level, while unsuccessful ones only affect a limited area.

---

**7) What is nullification?**  
Nullification is the idea that states have the right to invalidate any federal law they believe violates the Constitution. This concept has been historically controversial and was largely discredited following the Civil War and various Supreme Court rulings.

---

**8) What are enumerated or delegated powers?**  
Enumerated (or delegated) powers are those specifically granted to the federal government by the Constitution. Examples include the power to coin money, regulate interstate commerce, and maintain armed forces.

---

**9) What are implied powers?**  
Implied powers are those not explicitly stated in the Constitution but are inferred from the enumerated powers. They arise from the Necessary and Proper Clause, which allows the federal government to enact laws needed to execute its constitutionally assigned roles.

---

**10) Know the 10th Amendment**  
The 10th Amendment reserves powers not delegated to the federal government, nor prohibited to the states, for the states or the people. It serves as a constitutional foundation for state sovereignty and local autonomy.

---

**11) Know the following cases:**

- **a. Marbury v. Madison (1803):**  
  This case established the principle of judicial review, giving the Supreme Court the power to strike down laws that violate the Constitution.

- **b. McCulloch v. Maryland (1819):**  
  The Supreme Court ruled that Congress has implied powers under the Necessary and Proper Clause and that states cannot tax federal institutions, thereby reinforcing federal supremacy.

---

**12) Why are federal grants-in-aid important?**  
They provide significant funding for state and local programs, helping pay for services like education, healthcare, and infrastructure. Grants-in-aid also allow the federal government to influence state policy by attaching conditions to the funding.

---

**13) What are mandates?**  
Mandates are requirements that the federal government imposes on state and local governments. They often compel states to comply with certain standards or regulations, sometimes without providing corresponding funding (unfunded mandates).

---

**14) What is preemption?**  
Preemption occurs when federal law supersedes or overrides state or local laws in areas where the federal government has constitutional authority. It ensures uniform policy standards across all states in certain domains.

---

**15) Know what is usually contained in state constitutions (Bill of Rights, detailed policies, etc.)**  
State constitutions often include a bill of rights specific to the state, as well as more detailed policy guidelines regarding education, taxation, and local government structures. They can be much more extensive than the U.S. Constitution, addressing a wider range of state-specific issues.

---

**16) What is constitutionalism?**  
Constitutionalism is the principle that government authority is derived from a written constitution and that the government must operate within the confines of that constitution. It underlines the idea that no one is above the law, including government officials.

---

**17) Why are state constitutions long?**  
Many state constitutions are lengthy because they include detailed provisions on a wide array of policy areas. States often amend them more frequently to address specific issues or implement policy changes that at the federal level might be handled through legislation.

---

**18) From where do local governments derive their power?**  
Local governments derive their power from state constitutions and state laws. They operate under the authority granted to them by the state, which can structure and limit their responsibilities.

---

**19) How do most states amend their constitution?**  
Most states amend their constitutions through legislative proposals followed by voter approval in a referendum. Some also allow constitutional conventions or citizen initiatives as additional routes to propose amendments.

---

**20) Know the functions of counties and cities**  
Counties typically manage regional services like law enforcement in unincorporated areas, public records, and social services. Cities focus on providing local infrastructure, zoning regulations, and public utilities to their residents.

---

**21) What do special districts do?**  
Special districts provide specific services that are not handled by general-purpose governments, such as water supply, public transportation, or school districts. They can levy taxes or fees to fund their specialized operations.

---

**22) Know the different types of elections (at-large, single-member district, etc.)**  
- **At-large elections:** All voters in the jurisdiction vote for all seats up for election, meaning representatives serve the entire area.  
- **Single-member district elections:** The jurisdiction is divided into districts, and voters in each district choose one representative to serve their area.

---

**23) Know the different political cultures**  
Common categorizations include **moralistic** (emphasizing the public good and civic duty), **individualistic** (focusing on personal initiative and limited government), and **traditionalistic** (maintaining the status quo and existing social orders). These cultural types help explain variations in political attitudes and policy preferences across states.

---

**24) What is the political culture of Texas?**  
Texas is often described as having a mix of individualistic and traditionalistic political cultures. There is a strong emphasis on limited government, personal responsibility, and free markets, combined with traditional social values and respect for established hierarchies.

---

**25) What is the philosophy behind the current Texas Constitution?**  
The current Texas Constitution embodies a distrust of centralized authority and emphasizes limited government, separation of powers, and strong local control. It reflects the desire to protect individual rights and prevent abuse of power through detailed and restrictive provisions.

---

**26) Know the historical development of the Texas Constitution**  
Over time, Texas drafted several constitutions in response to major events like joining the United States (1845), seceding during the Civil War (1861), and Reconstruction (1866, 1869). The current constitution, adopted in 1876, was shaped by the desire to reduce government power and avoid abuses witnessed during Reconstruction.

---

**27) Know the political climate of the Constitutional Convention of 1875 (retrenchment and reform)**  
Delegates to the 1875 convention sought to reverse the perceived excesses of Reconstruction by limiting the powers of government. Their emphasis on “retrenchment and reform” led to strict budget constraints, shorter terms of office, and increased local autonomy.

---

**28) What are the differences between the Bill of Rights in the Texas and U.S. constitutions?**  
The Texas Bill of Rights is more extensive and is placed at the beginning of the constitution, reflecting its importance. It often includes more specific guarantees and protections than the U.S. Bill of Rights, such as explicit privacy provisions.

---

**29) What were the conditions of the annexation agreement for Texas to join the United States in 1845?**  
Texas retained the right to divide into multiple states if it chose, and it kept control of its public lands. Additionally, it joined the Union as a slave state, which influenced the balance of power in Congress at the time.

---

**30) What is the process to amend the Texas and U.S. Constitutions?**  
- **Texas Constitution:** Amendments usually require a proposal by the state legislature followed by voter approval in a statewide referendum.  
- **U.S. Constitution:** Amendments require a two-thirds vote in both houses of Congress and ratification by three-fourths of the states (or a constitutional convention called by two-thirds of states, though this method is rarely used).

---

**31) How has federalism evolved?**  
Federalism has shifted from a clear division of responsibilities (dual federalism) to more collaborative arrangements (cooperative federalism), and sometimes to forms of coercive federalism where the federal government strongly influences state policies through funding and regulations. Over time, the balance of power has generally moved toward increased national authority.

---

**32) Know the different types of federalism**  
- **Dual Federalism:** Clear separation of state and federal powers (layer cake federalism).  
- **Cooperative Federalism:** Overlapping functions and shared policy responsibilities (marble cake federalism).  
- **Coercive/Regulated Federalism:** The federal government sets rules for states, often withholding funds if conditions are not met.  
- **New Federalism:** Efforts to return some powers back to the states through block grants and devolution.

---

**33) Know different types of city governments (mayor-council, council-manager, etc.)**  
- **Mayor-Council:** Voters elect both a mayor (executive) and a council (legislative). The mayor may have strong or weak executive powers depending on the city charter.  
- **Council-Manager:** A city council is elected, but it hires a professionally trained city manager to handle daily administrative tasks and implement policies.

---

**34) Know the Sixteenth and Seventeenth Amendments**  
- **Sixteenth Amendment (1913):** Gave Congress the authority to levy an income tax without apportioning it among the states based on population.  
- **Seventeenth Amendment (1913):** Established the direct election of U.S. senators by the voters in each state, rather than by state legislatures.

---

**35) Who were the Radical Republicans?**  
They were a faction of the Republican Party during and after the Civil War committed to the abolition of slavery and the protection of African American rights. They led Reconstruction efforts, insisting on policies that would ensure civil rights and suffrage for freed slaves.

---

**36) What is a city charter?**  
A city charter functions like a constitution for a municipal government, outlining the structure, powers, and functions of the city’s government. It details how the city will be governed and can be changed through local referendums or other legal processes.

---

**37) What is eminent domain?**  
Eminent domain is the power of the government to take private property for public use, with fair compensation to the owner. It’s meant to allow governments to build infrastructure or provide public services while protecting property owners’ rights through compensation.

---

**38) What does home rule mean?**  
Home rule allows local governments to govern themselves with minimal state interference, as long as they adhere to the [[State Constitution]] and laws. This grants cities or counties greater autonomy to pass ordinances and manage local issues without seeking state approval for each decision.
